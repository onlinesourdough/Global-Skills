# Shape an offer

Produce a concise Offer Brief and the smallest useful validation. Use the
supplied customer evidence and delivery constraints to recommend an offer the
owner can sustain. This method's result is preparation; a larger authorized
task can continue afterward in its owning workflow.

## Decide from the evidence

Distinguish supplied or observed facts from reversible assumptions and unknowns
where they affect the decision. Keep source/date pointers when available. Do
not invent customer language, testimonials, market statistics, price benchmarks
or proof of demand. A founder belief remains a hypothesis.

Address what materially determines this offer: the customer and problem,
observable outcome, included work and exclusions, delivery/support boundaries,
customer responsibilities, price logic, costs and capacity. Use supplied numbers
for prices and economics; otherwise explain the missing decision. Consider time,
energy, attention, administration and financial constraints. Prefer a smaller
credible offer to unsupported promises, artificial scarcity or pressure.

Reuse settled inputs. Make ordinary reversible drafting choices without an
intake questionnaire. If conflicting or missing evidence could change the
customer, promise, price or delivery model, identify the most consequential
unknown and either ask one question or make it the validation target. Continue
with the parts that are supported; do not imply a contradiction is resolved.

## Return the usable brief

State the offer hypothesis, recommendation and reason, relevant delivery and
economic terms, boundaries, and distinguishable facts, assumptions and unknowns.
Choose the structure and detail the decision needs; do not create empty sections
or a second evidence document for a simple revision.

Propose one small validation of the main uncertainty: a reversible action,
observable signal, a time/effort/participant boundary based on supplied capacity,
and a learn/stop rule. A conversation or paid pilot can be a proposal without
being permission to contact anyone, collect payment or change operations.

Use `decision-ready` when no unresolved input could materially change the
proposed shape; that status does not claim the offer is market-proven. Otherwise
use `needs-one-material-decision` and name the frontier question or validation.
Check the recommendation against the evidence and capacity, correct in-scope
gaps, and return the brief without waiting for approval of routine drafting.
